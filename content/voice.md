# Voice guide

How Mohit writes. Drafts that do not sound like this get rewritten until they do.

## The job of a post

Someone hits a wall, searches, and lands here. In thirty seconds they should know
whether this post solves their problem. Everything below serves that.

## Titles — the single highest-leverage line

A title earns the click with a **concrete hook**: a number, a surprise, or a named
consequence. It must still be literally true.

| Weak | Strong |
| --- | --- |
| Fixing GitHub Pages deployment issues | Two errors, neither of them true: what GITHUB_TOKEN cannot do |
| Improving colour contrast | Your gradient button probably fails WCAG — mine hit 1.81:1 |
| Notes on theming in React | The flash of wrong theme, and why React alone cannot fix it |

Aim for 45–70 characters. Never a rhetorical question. Never "A Guide To".

## Summary — a pitch, not a synopsis

Two sentences, written to be read on a card in a feed. Sentence one: the situation and
the stakes. Sentence two: what the reader walks away with. It is the RSS description,
the social preview and the LinkedIn blurb, so it does more work than any other field.

## Structure — a scenario the reader can follow

1. **TL;DR** — one bolded line at the very top, the whole answer, no suspense
2. **The situation** — where this happened, what was being built, what was expected
3. **What broke** — the real error text, the real screenshot-in-words
4. **The obvious fix that fails** — and precisely why (usually the most useful part)
5. **The real cause** — explained mechanically, not hand-waved
6. **The fix** — working code, copy-pasteable
7. **What it generalises to** — two or three sentences, no "Conclusion" heading

Sub-heading every ~150 words. Short paragraphs — three sentences is plenty. At least one
code block, and a table wherever there is a comparison.

## Every post carries a cover image

Generated by `node scripts/make-cover.mjs <slug>` and committed with the post. Never
publish without one — it is what people see on LinkedIn, dev.to and in search.

Add **one** inline diagram only where it genuinely explains something a paragraph cannot
(a sequence, a before/after, a layout). Hand-written inline SVG, on the site's colours.
Decorative images are worse than none.

## Register

- First person, direct, concrete. "My deploy failed twice" — not "developers often
  encounter deployment issues".
- Specifics carry the post: exact error strings, exact numbers (`1.81:1`, `900px`,
  `0.0pt`), exact filenames. Vague claims get cut.
- Honest about mistakes and dead ends.
- Understated conclusions. Tell the reader what happened and what it means; never tell
  them to be excited.

## Banned

- "In today's fast-paced world", "game-changer", "delve", "leverage" as a verb, "unlock",
  "seamless", "robust" as filler, "dive in", "supercharge"
- Emoji in body text; hashtag blocks; "Conclusion" as a heading
- Rhetorical-question openers
- Unverifiable claims ("10x faster") — every number traceable to a real measurement
- Selling anything. The work is the pitch.

specificity. Match its density; beat its structure using the seven-part shape above.
