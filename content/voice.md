# Voice guide

How Mohit writes. Drafts that do not sound like this get rewritten until they do.

## The voice

- First person, direct, concrete. "My deploy failed twice" — not "developers often
  encounter deployment issues".
- Plain sentences. One idea each. Contractions are fine.
- Specifics carry the post: exact error messages, exact numbers (`1.81:1`, `900px`,
  `0.0pt`), exact file names. Vague claims get cut.
- Honest about mistakes and dead ends — the wrong turn is usually the most useful part
  of the story.
- Understated conclusions. The reader is told what happened and what it means; they are
  never told to be excited.

## Structure that works

1. Open with the problem as it actually appeared — the error text, the broken screen
2. The wrong obvious fix, if there was one, and why it fails
3. The real cause, explained mechanically
4. The fix, with code
5. A short generalisable takeaway — two or three sentences, no heading like "Conclusion"

## Banned

- "In today's fast-paced world", "game-changer", "delve", "leverage" as a verb,
  "unlock", "seamless", "robust" as filler
- Emoji in body text; hashtag blocks
- Rhetorical questions as openers ("Have you ever wondered…?")
- Unverifiable claims ("10x faster") — every number must be traceable to a real
  measurement
- Selling anything. The work is the pitch.
